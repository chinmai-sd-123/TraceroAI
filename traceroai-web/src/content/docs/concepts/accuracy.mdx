## The numbers

Measured on 117 labelled traces (22 healthy answers, 18 correct refusals, 20 retrieval
misses, 21 unsupported claims, 36 wrong answers):

| Tier | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| Term overlap | 58.1% (68 of 117) |
| Embedding (`text-embedding-3-small`) | 76.1% (89 of 117) |
| LLM judge (`gpt-5.4-nano`) | 94.0–95.7% over three runs |

The judge gave the same label in all three runs for 96.6% of the cases. The
[full report](https://github.com/chinmai-sd-123/TraceroAI/blob/main/docs/evaluation-benchmark.md)
has precision and recall per label, and every verdict.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Term overlap** rarely recognises a correct answer worded differently from the
  source (3 of 22 healthy answers), and labels most refusals correct even when the
  context held the answer.
- **Embeddings** are mostly undecided rather than wrong: 21 of their 28 misses are
  "needs review".
- **The judge** sometimes calls a reworded claim unsupported ("outside auditors" for
  "third-party auditors").

## Kept honest

The benchmark runs every week in CI against the real judge and fails if judge accuracy
or self-agreement drops below 90%. You can run it yourself:

```bash
cd services/api
python -m app.eval.benchmark --repeat 3 --report report.json
```

It makes real provider calls (a few cents with a small model).

## Experiment grading is separate

[Experiments](/docs/fix/experiments) grade answers against expected answers with a
single judge call. Checked by hand, that grader disagreed with a
careful reading on about 1 in 6 answers, mostly by failing long answers that were
right. Treat small differences between experiment variants with care, and read the
answers when a result decides something.
