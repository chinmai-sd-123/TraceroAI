`log_retrieval` (and `log_trace`) accept your retriever's output in the forms retrievers
usually return it, so you rarely need to convert anything.

| You pass | It becomes |
|---|---|
| A dict with a `text` field | A chunk with that text and the dict's other fields |
| A string | A chunk with that text |
| A LangChain `Document` | A chunk with its `page_content`, and these metadata keys mapped across: `id` or `chunk_id`, `document_id` or `doc_id`, `title` or `document_title`, `source`, `section`, and `score` (as `final_score`) |

Missing `rank`s and `chunk_id`s are filled in from position, and chunks with no text are
left out, so a trace is never rejected because of a chunk's shape.

## Fields worth sending

```python
t.log_retrieval([
    {
        "text": "Refunds take 5 to 7 business days.",
        "document_id": "refunds.md",       # used to group retrieval failures into issues
        "document_title": "Refund policy",
        "section": "Timing",
        "final_score": 0.82,               # your retriever's score, shown on the trace
    },
    ...
], strategy="hybrid", config={"final_top_k": 3})
```

The first chunk's `document_id` (or `source`, or `document_title`) names the issue a
retrieval failure joins, so send one if you can. Scores are shown as you send them;
TraceroAI doesn't invent any.

## Limits

At most 200 chunks per trace, and the whole request must fit
`TRACEROAI_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES` (1 MB by default). Send fewer or shorter chunks, or raise
the limit.
